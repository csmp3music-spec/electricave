# Historical Boston Expansion - 2026-03-29

This pass added two groups of content:

1. A stronger Boston core civic layer with a circa-1900 skyline read, the Massachusetts State House, Boston Common, the Public Garden, the Commonwealth Avenue Mall, and a Longfellow Bridge approach.
2. New inferred surface/interurban services off the Tremont subway core:
   - `Boston Northern Interurban - Salem, NH`
   - `Boston Northern Interurban - Canobie Lake Park`
   - `Boston Northern Interurban - Whalom Park`
   - `South Shore Portal - Nantasket Beach / Paragon Park`

## Research basis

- Public Garden and Boston Common:
  - Boston.gov, `Public Garden`
  - https://www.boston.gov/parks/public-garden
  - Used for the contrast between the 1634 Common and the 1837 Public Garden, plus the Garden's ornamental/Victorian character.
- Commonwealth Avenue Mall:
  - The Cultural Landscape Foundation, `Commonwealth Avenue Mall`
  - https://www.tclf.org/landscapes/commonwealth-avenue-mall
  - Used for the Back Bay axial boulevard and mall alignment between the Public Garden and Massachusetts Avenue.
- Massachusetts State House:
  - Massachusetts Secretary of the Commonwealth, `An Online Tour of the Massachusetts State House`
  - https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/state-house-tours/trsbok/grehall.htm
  - Used to anchor the Bulfinch-era State House as a dominant Beacon Hill landmark.
- Tremont Street Subway / Pleasant Street and Public Garden portals:
  - CelebrateBoston `Green Line Historic Images`
  - https://www.celebrateboston.com/mbta/green-line/tremont-street-subway.htm
  - Tremont Street subway / Pleasant Street incline reference pages
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tremont_Street_subway
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleasant_Street_incline
  - Used for the Park Street/Boylston portal logic and the explicit Boylston-to-Pleasant Street south portal concept.
- Longfellow Bridge:
  - Longfellow Bridge reference page
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longfellow_Bridge
  - Used for the 1900-1906 bridge timing and the bridge's streetcar-plus-rail reservation history.
- Northern interurban / South Shore network framing:
  - 1909 map of the Boston and Northern Street Railway and Old Colony Street Railway
  - https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:1909_map_of_the_Boston_and_Northern_Street_Railway_and_Old_Colony_Street_Railway.jpg
  - Used to justify the Boston & Northern / Old Colony network envelope for the new surface through-routes.
- Canobie Lake Park:
  - Concord Monitor, `Granite State Stories: Canobie Lake Park opens its doors`
  - https://www.concordmonitor.com/2018/06/19/canobie-lake-park-opens-its-doors-17980724/
  - Nashua City Station, `Canobie Lake Park Station`
  - https://www.nashuacitystation.org/station/new-hampshire/rockingham/salem/canobie-lake-park/
  - Used because the park opened in `1902` as a trolley park and the Salem area had documented early-1900s electric service tied to the park and Lowell/Lawrence routes.
- Whalom Park:
  - New Whalom historical notes
  - https://newwhalom.com/parks-across-america/
  - Used for the Fitchburg & Leominster Street Railway / Whalom Park trolley-park relationship.
- Nantasket / Paragon Park:
  - Paragon Park reference page
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paragon_Park
  - Used because Paragon Park opened in `1905` at Nantasket Beach, making it an early-20th-century leisure terminus rather than a strict `1900` landmark.

## Implementation notes

- The civic skyline is intentionally low-rise. It emphasizes the State House dome, Common/Public Garden open space, early downtown massing, and a few late-19th-century vertical accents rather than a modern skyline.
- The northern and South Shore services are playable inferred through-routes. They are not strict stop-for-stop reconstructions of a single 1900 operating company.
- `Canobie Lake Park (1902)` and `Paragon Park (1905)` are later than the skyline target year. They were added because the user explicitly asked for those trolley-park terminals.
- `Black Falcon / Commonwealth Pier` is included as a South Boston waterfront orientation stop. The name bridges a later site label onto an earlier waterfront corridor.
